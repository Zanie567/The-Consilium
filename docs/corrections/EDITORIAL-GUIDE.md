# Corrections: a short guide for editors

## Which kind of change is it?

| If… | Use | Readers see |
|---|---|---|
| A fact was wrong when we published it | **Correction** | The corrected text, a notice at the top, a dated note at the end |
| It was not wrong, but could mislead or lacked context | **Clarification** | A dated note labelled *Clarification* |
| Something new has happened since | **Update** | A dated note labelled *Update* (never called an error) |
| Spelling, punctuation, formatting; the meaning is unchanged | **Minor copy edit** | Nothing; it is recorded internally |

If in doubt between a copy edit and a correction, it is a correction.

## Issuing a correction

1. **Editorial → Articles**, find the published article, click **Corrections** (or **Manage corrections** in the Review
   Studio or the article editor).
2. **New correction, clarification or update.**
3. Choose the type.
4. Record what the article said and what is correct. This is the internal record.
5. Fix the article text in the editor on the page. If the cover picture was wrong or misleading, replace it here; if
   the article was credited to the wrong person, change the byline here. "Your changes to the live article" shows
   exactly what will change.
6. Write the public note. **Draft it from step 2** gives you the house wording to edit, for example:
   *An earlier version of this article stated that unemployment had risen to 5.2%. The correct figure was 4.8%. The
   article has been amended.*
7. Add sources and who raised it under **Evidence and internal context**. Readers never see this.
8. **Show preview** shows the article and the note exactly as readers will see them.
9. Editors: **Submit for approval…**. Administrators: **Publish correction…**, or **Save for approval later**.
   You are asked to confirm before anything goes live.

Nothing you type is lost if a save fails: the form keeps it, and also keeps a copy on this device.

## Approving (administrators)

The article's Manage corrections page shows **Awaiting approval** with the note, the internal context and the exact text
changes. **Approve and publish…** makes the text change and the note live together, with today's date and your name as
approver. **Revise** changes it first; **Withdraw** (with a reason) drops it but keeps it in the history.

If the article changed after the correction was prepared, approval is refused: revise the correction against the
current text first. Only one correction can await approval per article.

## After publication

- Published notes are **permanent**. If a note is itself wrong, use **Amend this note** in the history; readers see the
  amendment beneath the original, linked to it.
- The article keeps its original publication date. Each note carries its own date.
- The author is notified in the editorial portal. No email or newsletter is sent.
- The history on the Manage corrections page lists every notice (published, pending, withdrawn), who prepared and
  approved it, and every minor copy edit since publication.

## Live articles in the editor

A published article is no longer saved automatically. **Save changes** asks you to confirm that the change is a minor
copy edit (spelling, punctuation, formatting, or a better version of the same picture); anything else goes through
Manage corrections. The web address and the byline of a published article cannot be changed in the editor.

## Removing an article

Deleting an article permanently (from the trash, or automatically 30 days after it was trashed) also removes its
correction notes from the site. Their text is kept in the internal audit log. The public policy says so.

## Writers

If you spot a possible error in your own published article, open it in the editor, select the passage and leave a
comment. The editors responsible are notified. Readers report errors through the methods on the public
[Corrections Policy](/corrections) page.
